# Boxer Shorts {#boxer-shorts}

- **Aliases**: boxers, men's boxer shorts, loose underwear shorts; fr: caleçon, boxer-short
- **Family**: menswear underwear (woven)
- **Recognize**: loose woven shorts, usually in cotton poplin; elastic waistband, an overlapped fly with a button, side seams, a CB seam, inseam, no gusset.
- **Pieces**: front (2, left and right), back (2, left and right with CB seam), fly facing (1), fly overlap (1), waist elastic (1), waistband casing (1) if separate, button (1).
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified numbers. A short trouser shape: the front crotch curve is shorter and shallower, the back crotch curve longer and deeper (as in trousers, see skirts-trousers technique sheet). Fly: the right front carries a fly extension 3 to 4 cm wide (typical), the left front a fly facing; the opening reaches about 10 to 14 cm below the waist (not a source value). Side seams and inseams straight. Leg width at the hem is wide (full circumference about 56 to 64 cm, typical guess). Right front fly extension is stitched to the left front along the CF; the fly lap closes with one button at the waist.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take hip, waist, rise. 2. Block with 10 to 16 cm ease at the hip (typical for a loose boxer). 3. Crotch depth = body rise + 3 to 5 cm (typical). 4. Leg length 10 to 25 cm below the crotch for a mid-thigh short. 5. Add fly extension and facing. 6. Waist: elastic casing about 3 cm, elastic 25 to 35 mm at about 0.75 to 0.85 of the waist (typical).
- **Sewing order**: back CB seam, fly, front crotch seam, side seams, inseam, waist casing, elastic, hems, button.
- **Finishes and notions**: French or flat-fell seams, 25 to 35 mm woven elastic, 1 button.
- **Fabrics**: cotton poplin, flannel, silk; woven with no stretch, so crotch depth matters.
- **Fit faults**: crotch pulls: deepen the crotch curve by 1 to 2 cm; legs ride: reduce hem width.
- **Variants**: #boxer-briefs, #pajama-trousers.
- **CLO3D**: woven preset, mesh 10 mm; model the overlap fly as layered pieces.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; numbers typical.
- **Status**: unverified
