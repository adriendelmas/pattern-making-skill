# Newborn Bodysuit (envelope neck, snap crotch) {#newborn-bodysuit}

- **Aliases**: baby vest, onesie, lap-shoulder bodysuit, body (fr), envelope shoulder bodysuit; fr: body bébé, body à encolure américaine (cache-coeur à épaules croisées), body naissance
- **Family**: babywear (knit)
- **Recognize**: a one-piece knit baby garment that closes at the crotch with 3 snaps; shoulders with an envelope (lapped) neckline: the front and back shoulder edges overlap so the neck opening can stretch over the baby's relatively large head; neck and leg openings finished with binding or rib; sleeves long or short, sometimes with fold-over mitts.
- **Pieces**: front (1), back (1), sleeve (2), neck binding (1), leg bindings (2), sleeve cuffs (2), snap tape or snaps (3 to 5 crotch), optional snaps at shoulders (not on an envelope neck).
- **Shape**: text source (Müller & Sohn, size 74 basic body block): a baby's head is large relative to the neck, so the neckline needs extra opening; the lapped (overlapping) shoulder is a simple and convenient variant that provides enough width for the head, avoiding pressure on sensitive skin; steps include lowering the armhole 1 cm and drawing the curved sleeve seam; the lapped shoulder is made by drawing the new shoulder seam and mirroring it over the shoulder on both the front and back pieces. For the wrap variant, they trace the front pattern and mirror it over CF to get the wrap overlap and mark snap positions. Overlap width, snap spacing, ease and crotch are not given by that source (unverified). Trade practice for the envelope neck: each shoulder edge is extended by an extension of about 2 to 3 cm (techniques 11.5 gives 2.5 for the overlap; typical) toward the arm side: the front's extension laps over the back's (or the reverse), neck edges curved so the closed neckline stays round, the extension edges are finished with a narrow hem; the sleeve cap sits within the overlapped area. Crotch: the back piece extends below the crotch as a flap about 4 to 6 cm long (typical), the front crotch ends as a similar flap; flaps overlap by 2.5 to 3 cm (techniques 5.8) and carry the snaps. Torso loop T (techniques 5.8) governs the length.
- **Drafting**: 1. Use a knit baby bodysuit block, size by height (e.g. 50 to 62 cm for newborn to 3 months, EN 13402 height sizes; Ottobre sizes in 6 cm steps start at 50). 2. Torso length = T x (1 - V), V 3 to 5 percent for 50 to 70 percent vertical stretch (techniques 5.8); plus 1 to 2 cm of growth allowance in the torso length (a typical practice, not from a source). 3. Armhole lowered by 1 cm and the neckline widened for the head (Müller & Sohn). 4. Shoulder overlaps of 2 to 3 cm. 5. Crotch width 8 to 10 cm (techniques 5.8), crotch flap overlap 2.5 to 3 cm. 6. Neck binding at 0.80 to 0.90 of the neck edge (techniques 3.1 style). 7. Check that the closed neck circumference stretched is at least the newborn head circumference plus ease (head measurement to be taken or checked against the size chart).
- **Sewing order**: sleeves flat to the front and back, hem the shoulder overlaps, side/sleeve seams in one pass, neck binding, leg binding, cuffs, crotch snaps.
- **Finishes and notions**: plastic or metal snaps rated for baby garments (3 to 5 at the crotch), narrow binding, coverstitch for hems, flat seams away from the skin, tagless label.
- **Fabrics**: cotton interlock, jersey with 3 to 5 percent elastane, rib; soft and washable; EN safety requirements for small parts apply.
- **Fit faults**: neck too tight for the head: increase overlap and neck width; crotch snaps pull: lengthen torso; shoulders gape: shorten overlap.
- **Variants**: #romper, #sleepsuit-with-feet, #bodysuit for adults.
- **CLO3D**: mesh 4 to 6 mm; an infant avatar; build overlaps as layered shoulder pieces; the closure snaps are not simulated; check the neckline on the avatar head if available.
