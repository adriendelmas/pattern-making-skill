# French Knickers {#french-knickers}

- **Aliases**: tap pants, wide-leg knickers, silk knickers; fr: culotte française, culotte ample
- **Family**: lingerie, briefs (woven or soft knit)
- **Recognize**: loose, wide legs that flare slightly; generally in silk, satin or crepe; elastic waist or a yoke; side seams, sometimes a side slit; lace at the leg opening; no tight leg elastic.
- **Pieces**: front (1), back (1), crotch gusset (optional, 1), waist elastic or yoke (1), lace trim.
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified numbers. In woven fabric the front and back are short loose trouser shapes: each a trapezoid, narrower at the waist, wide at the hem, with a curved crotch line; the crotch seam and the inseam are one continuous curve from front to back (or a gusset joins the two crotch curves). Crotch depth is the key: the crotch curve length of a woven garment is greater than in a stretch brief and must be tested on a toile. A bias cut gives drape.
- **Drafting**: 1. Take hip, waist, body rise and thigh circumference; use hip + 6 to 10 cm ease for a woven (typical). 2. Draft a short trouser-like block with crotch depth = body rise + 2 to 3 cm (typical). 3. Flare the side seam by 2 to 4 cm at the hem. 4. Add a 1.5 cm waist casing for elastic, or a yoke. 5. Seam allowance 1.0 cm with French seams.
- **Sewing order**: side seams (French seams), crotch seam, waist casing or yoke, hems (narrow rolled hem or lace).
- **Finishes and notions**: 8 to 12 mm waist elastic or a yoke with a hook, lace or rolled hems.
- **Fabrics**: silk charmeuse, satin, habotai, viscose crepe; on the bias the pieces drop up to 3 cm (techniques 5.8).
- **Fit faults**: bunching at the crotch: reduce crotch depth by 1 cm; slides down: raise the waist or narrow the yoke.
- **Variants**: #basic-brief, #boyshort, #chemise.
- **CLO3D**: woven fabric preset, mesh 8 mm; hang the bias before finishing.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; numbers typical.
- **Status**: unverified
