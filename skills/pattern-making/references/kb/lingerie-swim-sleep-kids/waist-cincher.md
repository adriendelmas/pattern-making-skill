# Waist Cincher {#waist-cincher}

- **Aliases**: waist trainer, waspie, girdle belt, corset belt, elastic cincher; fr: serre-taille, guêpière courte (partial), gaine serre-taille
- **Family**: corsetry, foundations
- **Recognize**: short (about 15 to 25 cm tall) band covering waist and upper hip with several bones; closure by 2 or 3 rows of hooks at CF (hook busk) or by lacing; often in rubber latex or powernet with rigid vertical bones.
- **Pieces**: panels (6 to 10, often cut as a front and back with side seams), lining, hook-and-eye or hook busk (1), CB lacing or elastic, bone casings, binding, hook tape.
- **Shape**: trade practice, unverified. A cylinder with waist smaller than hip: each side panel a slightly curved vertical strip that narrows at the waist and widens toward the top and bottom like a corset panel, but with less hip spring since the cincher is short. The bone channels follow the panel seams; steel bones at CF and CB closures, plastic or spiral steel at curves. A hook busk at CF (rows of hooks on one side, eyes on the other) closes it.
- **Drafting**: as #corset-modern but shorter: height from 15 to 25 cm (typical), centred on the waist; reduction 5 to 10 cm for comfort (techniques 5.9); stretch fabrics use the negative-ease method of techniques 6.6 with a 15 to 25 percent reduction; bone count 6 to 12.
- **Sewing order**: panels, bone channels, bones, closure, binding.
- **Finishes and notions**: hook-and-eye tape 3 rows, flat or spiral steel bones, powernet panels, latex alternatives.
- **Fabrics**: powernet, elastic coutil, latex; non-stretch bones.
- **Fit faults**: rolls at the waist: band too short; bones dig: round the ends or lower the bone count.
- **Variants**: #corset-modern, #shapewear.
- **CLO3D**: stretchy fabric with strong negative ease; check strain at the waist; bones as stiffer strips.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; panels and heights typical, outlines unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
