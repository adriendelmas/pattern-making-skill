# Gun flap {#gun-flap}

- **Aliases**: storm flap (shoulder), right-shoulder flap, shooting patch; fr: patte d'épaule droite, rabat de tir, empiècement
- **Family**: outerwear components
- **Recognize**: a flap or patch over the right upper chest and shoulder of a trench coat or shooting jacket, sewn at the shoulder and armhole only, lower edge free and buttoned
- **Pieces**: outer flap (1), under flap/facing of the same shape (1), interfacing
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 11.3 step 4).
  A flap on the right front starting at the shoulder seam and covering the upper chest, 14 to 18 wide at the shoulder, with a straight lower edge 12 to 14 below the armhole level, buttoned at its end. It is sewn in at the shoulder and the armhole only; the lower edge hangs free over a facing of the same shape. The shoulder end is sewn into the shoulder seam; the armhole end into the armhole seam.
- **Drafting**: trace the right front above the chest line, add the flap; shoulder width 14 to 18, depth 12 to 14 below the armhole level; cut outer and under flap, interface the outer; add button and buttonhole at the end
- **Sewing order**: sew outer and under flap right sides together except at the top, turn, press, topstitch, baste into the shoulder and armhole seams, then sew the sleeve
- **Finishes and notions**: button 22 to 28 mm, double topstitching
- **Fabrics**: gabardine, waterproof cotton; flap should be of the same cloth as the coat
- **Fit faults**: flap lifts at the end -> button 1 to 1.5 from the end; flap catches on the sleeve -> shorten the armhole end
- **Variants**: #storm-flap, #trench-coat, #epaulette
- **CLO3D**: separate piece sewn at the shoulder and armhole; sleeve sewing after
- **Confidence**: TO 11.3 (trade practice, Minerva trench anatomy); outline unverified
- **Status**: unverified
