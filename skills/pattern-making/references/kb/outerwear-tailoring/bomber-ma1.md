# Bomber / flight jacket (MA-1) {#bomber-ma1}

- **Aliases**: MA-1, bomber jacket, flight jacket, blouson; fr: blouson aviateur, bomber, MA-1
- **Family**: casual and military outerwear
- **Recognize**: short blousing jacket in nylon, rib knit collar, cuffs and waistband, front zip, two slash or welt pockets, a zipped utility pocket on the left sleeve (MA-1), often a reversible rescue-orange lining; the spec history is MIL-J-8279 with revisions to G in 1988 (Wikipedia, Buzz Rickson page)
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (1 or 2), sleeve (2, set-in, often 2-piece with a slight curve), rib collar (1, 2 wide), rib cuffs (2), hem rib (1, joined at the side, or 2), facing (2, 3 to 4 wide) or zip tape, welt pockets (2) + bags (4) with or without zip, sleeve pocket (1), lining front (2), back (1), sleeve (2), zip (TO 16.1)
- **Shape**: Source: unverified as an outline (TO 16.1 gives the piece set and rib numbers; no drawn pattern read).
  Short straight body, nape-to-hem 50 to 62; hem sweep = body hip + 8 to 14 so the body blouses over the hem rib; front zip without facing or with a narrow 3 to 4 facing; shoulder dropped 1.5 to 3; underarm lowered 3 to 5. Rib bands: finished depth 6 to 8 for hem, cuff and collar; the band length is 75 to 85 percent of the edge it joins for a 100 percent stretch rib (85 to 90 with elastane); blouse ratio body edge / rib circumference 1.15 to 1.30; neckline rib at about 0.75 of the neck edge, sleeve and body hem at about 0.90 in the Ottobre rule. Sleeve pocket: a zip pocket on the upper left sleeve, size not known.
- **Drafting**: TO 16.1: ease 22 to 32 (woven nylon, worn over knit), rib bands quartered and stretched evenly, zip set before the hem rib
- **Sewing order**: TO 16.1: assemble body, sleeves and lining; sew the rib bands joined at the ends; attach them with the stretch evenly distributed (quarter marks), with zip stops at the ends of the front rib; attach the lining; finish with the collar rib; the zip is set before the hem rib
- **Finishes and notions**: knit rib with 5 to 10 percent elastane, metal or coil zip No. 5, lining polyester wadding
- **Fabrics**: nylon, polyester, satin; knit rib stretches and shrinks, test a loop on the body first
- **Fit faults**: rib wavy -> cut shorter or use stretch tape; hem blouses too much -> reduce the hem sweep; cuff too tight -> increase the rib length
- **Variants**: #harrington-jacket, #windbreaker, #varsity-jacket, #puffer-jacket
- **CLO3D**: rib as a separate stretchy fabric with strain ratio 80 to 85 percent; body as nylon; mesh 6 to 8 mm
- **Confidence**: TO 16.1 (trade practice, Ottobre and sewing.org rib pages); MA-1 spec background from Wikipedia and the Buzz Rickson page; outline unverified
- **Status**: unverified
