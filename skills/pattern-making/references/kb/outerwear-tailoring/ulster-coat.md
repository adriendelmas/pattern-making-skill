# Ulster coat {#ulster-coat}

- **Aliases**: Ulster overcoat, Ulster, ulster with cape (historic); fr: ulster, manteau ulster
- **Family**: coats, overcoats
- **Recognize**: heavy, long, loose overcoat, often double-breasted and belted, in rugged cloth (herringbone tweed); the historic version had a short cape reaching just past the elbow which was dropped from the 1890s on
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (2 or 1), sleeve (2 or 2+2), collar (1), undercollar (1 to 2), facing (2), pockets (flap or patch), belt (1), belt loops, cape (1, historic, optional), lining
- **Shape**: Source: unverified (secondary descriptions: Wikipedia Ulster coat, The London Lounge forum; no drawn pattern read).
  Loose straight body, DB or SB, long length (mid-calf to knee), belted version with a full belt at the waist or a half belt at the back (the half belt is a forum remark, not verified). Collar large and convertible (can be raised) in some versions (unverified). Historic cape: a piece reaching just past the elbows, attached at the neck and shoulder seam, hem straight or scalloped (not known).
- **Drafting**: as #overcoat with the ease 26 to 34, hem sweep 150 to 180, length 105 to 125 (typical, unverified for Ulster), belt 5 to 6 wide, belt loops at the side seams; DB wrap 8 to 10
- **Sewing order**: as #overcoat, belt and loops at the end
- **Finishes and notions**: large buttons 30 to 35 mm, belt buckle, lining wool or plaid
- **Fabrics**: heavy tweed, Donegal, melton; check/herringbone match at the fronts
- **Fit faults**: bulk at the waist -> belt loops positioned on the sides; coat drags on the legs -> shorten or add a vent
- **Variants**: #overcoat, #chesterfield-coat, #polo-coat, #cape
- **CLO3D**: heavy fabric property; belt as a separate piece; cape as an extra layer sewn at the shoulder
- **Confidence**: descriptive sources only; numbers extrapolated from TO 10; unverified
- **Status**: unverified
