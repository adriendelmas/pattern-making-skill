# Blind hem machine {#machine-blind-hem}

- **Aliases**: blindstitch machine, blind stitcher, hemmer, blind hem stitch (stitch 103 single-thread blindstitch); fr: point invisible, machine à ourlet invisible, ourleuse
- **Family**: machines
- **Recognize**: a hem with no stitches visible on the right side, only a small dimple on the face at intervals; the hem edge is tucked and the curved needle bites the shell only partly
- **Pieces**: no special piece; hem allowance folded under or with the edge finished
- **Shape**: hem allowance 2.5 to 4 cm (trousers and skirts); the fold is made "S"-shaped (folded back on itself so that only the fold, not the edge, is penetrated). Use a hem allowance of at least 2.5 cm to get the machine bite. Source: unverified.
- **Drafting**: hem 3 to 4 cm; finish the edge first; fold the hem and then fold back.
- **Sewing order**: finish edge, press hem fold, machine blind hem, press without steam on the face.
- **Finishes and notions**: single-thread chainstitch, curved needle (C-type); a blind hem foot on a home machine makes a lockstitch zigzag blind hem (stitch 306 type).
- **Fabrics**: wool, trousers, suit skirts, lined skirts; stable fabrics; not for sheers.
- **Fit faults**: stitches show on the face -> set a shallower bite; hem comes undone -> the chain breaks and unravels, backstitch the end.
- **Variants**: hand hem (#hem-couture, #catch-stitch).
- **CLO3D**: not modelled.
- **Confidence**: trade practice.
- **Status**: unverified
