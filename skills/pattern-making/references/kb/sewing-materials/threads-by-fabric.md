# Threads by fabric {#threads-by-fabric}

- **Aliases**: sewing threads, thread weights, tex; fr: fils à coudre, titrage
- **Family**: machine accessories
- **Recognize**: a thread is described by fibre, construction and weight (Tex number: higher = thicker)
- **Pieces**: no special piece
- **Shape**: ST section 9 gives Tex 40 to 60 for most wovens, Tex 70 to 100 for topstitching; fine silks Tex 27 to 30; heavy denim and leather Tex 40 to 70 for seams. Source: ST (industrial notes); fine-weight values from memory.
- **Drafting**: not applicable.
- **Sewing order**: not applicable.
- **Finishes and notions**: all-purpose polyester or cotton-wrapped polyester (wovens); 100% cotton (cotton, linen; breaks less on pressing; weak in stretch); silk (silk, wool, basting, pick stitch; fine, easy to press); nylon bonded (leather, upholstery, outdoor); buttonhole twist and topstitch thread (topstitching, buttonholes); wooly nylon (looper thread on knits and swimwear); monofilament (invisible hems); textured polyester (cover stitch).
- **Fabrics**: match the fibre of thread to the fabric; never use a thread stronger than the fabric on loose weaves (it cuts through).
- **Fit faults**: seams pucker -> thread too thick; seams break in stretch -> use a stretch thread and stitch.
- **Variants**: #needles-by-fabric.
- **CLO3D**: not relevant.
- **Confidence**: trade practice.
- **Status**: practice
