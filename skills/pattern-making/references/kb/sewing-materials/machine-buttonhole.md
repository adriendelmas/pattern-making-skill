# Buttonhole machine {#machine-buttonhole}

- **Aliases**: buttonholer, keyhole buttonhole machine, eyelet buttonhole, lockstitch buttonhole; fr: boutonnière, machine à boutonnières
- **Family**: machines
- **Recognize**: a zigzag-sewn slit with a bar at one or both ends; a keyhole end with a round eye on tailored and workwear garments; straight bar-ended holes on shirts; lockstitch machines (301-based) are the common type
- **Pieces**: no special piece; buttonhole area is interfaced (4 to 5 cm wide patch, ST section 4.6)
- **Shape**: length = button diameter + thickness (ST section 5.2); position, extension beyond the CF and orientation follow ST section 5.2 (keyhole eye toward the garment edge on horizontal holes). Source: unverified here (ST section 5.2 is the reference).
- **Drafting**: place the buttonholes on the pattern as lines with the button position at the CF.
- **Sewing order**: stitch buttonholes after interfacing is fused and before the final press; cut after stitching.
- **Finishes and notions**: gimp cord for tailored holes; fine thread; buttonhole twist for hand-worked holes.
- **Fabrics**: woven shirts, jackets; leather and faux leather use a bound or eyelet buttonhole.
- **Fit faults**: buttonhole gaps -> bar too short; frays -> stitch width too small.
- **Variants**: hand-worked buttonhole, bound buttonhole (ST section 5.2).

- **Confidence**: trade practice.
- **Status**: unverified
