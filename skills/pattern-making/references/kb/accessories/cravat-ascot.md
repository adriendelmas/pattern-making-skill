# Cravat / Ascot {#cravat-ascot}

- **Aliases**: day cravat, ascot tie, Ascot, neck scarf; fr: cravate ascot, lavaliere, foulard de cou
- **Family**: neckwear, wide
- **Recognize**: a wide soft silk piece folded at the neck with a pleated front, ends tucked into the shirt; ascot has two wide pointed ends overlapped and secured with a pin.
- **Pieces**: for a day cravat: tapered strip with two wide ends (x2 layers); optional lining (x1); the ascot: two wider ends (x2) and a narrow neck section (x1).
- **Shape**: unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction logic: a tapered strip, wide at both ends (about 15 to 20) and narrow in the middle (about 8 to 10, unverified), length about 1500 to 1800 for a day cravat, shorter for a pre-tied ascot (unverified). Ends are pointed or squared, sometimes pleated (the "pleated ascot"). Seam the two layers right sides together, turn, press.
- **Drafting**: 1. Length from the neck girth + 700 to 900 (unverified). 2. Taper to the neck width. 3. SA 6 to 10.
- **Sewing order**: sew long sides, turn through the end, close by hand.
- **Finishes and notions**: hand-rolled edges, a stud or pin.
- **Fabrics**: silk, silk twill, wool challis.
- **Fit faults**: bulky (use a thinner silk), slips (fix with a pin).
- **Variants**: #necktie, #bow-tie, #scarf.
- **CLO3D**: draped strip.
- **Confidence**: from memory; unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
