# Bag Lining and Interfacing {#bag-lining-interfacing}

- **Aliases**: bag stabilizer, fusible fleece, foam, bag interior; fr: doublure et entoilage de sac
- **Family**: technique, bag structure
- **Recognize**: invisible from outside; visible as inner pocket, stiff shape, soft or hard bottom.
- **Pieces**: lining (same panels as the outer, x2 or per piece), interfacing (same as the outer, trimmed), pocket (x1 or more), bottom board (x1), stiffener (optional).
- **Shape**: derived. Lining pieces mirror the outer pieces; for a lined tote, the lining is cut 10 shorter in height (technique sheet 3.1) so it does not pull the rim; the lining is not interfaced or is lightly interfaced. Interfacing is cut at the stitching line minus 5 to 10 on all edges (to reduce bulk at seams): fusible fleece for body, woven fusible for stability, foam for padded bags. Bottom board: rectangle W - 20 x D - 20 [T]. Patch pocket 150 x 180 [T].
- **Drafting**: 1. Trace outer pieces. 2. Reduce lining height 10. 3. Trim interfacing 5 to 10 from the edge. 4. Mark pocket position. 5. Cut the bottom board smaller than the base.
- **Sewing order**: fuse the interfacing, sew the lining separately with a 120 turning gap, join the lining to the outer at the top.
- **Finishes and notions**: fusible interfacing, foam, bottom board, rigid plastic.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, polyester, nylon lining; match weight to the outer.
- **Fit faults**: lining sags (cut too long), outer puckers (interfacing too stiff or wrinkled).
- **Variants**: #tote-bag, #gusseted-tote, #backpack.
- **CLO3D**: lining as extra layer or omitted.
- **Confidence**: trade practice, technique sheet 3.1.
