# Facings and linings derived {#facings-and-linings-derived}

- **Aliases**: facing pattern, lining pattern, shaped facing, all-in-one facing; fr: parementure, doublure
- **Family**: manipulation (finishing patterns)
- **Recognize**: the inside finishing pieces that follow a garment edge.
- **Pieces**: front facing (2), back neck facing (1), armhole facing (2), lining front (2), lining back
  (1 on fold or 2), lining sleeve (2).
- **Shape**: facing: copy the edge to face over a width of 4 to 6 cm, 1 to 2 mm narrower so the seam
  rolls inwards (transformations.md). The facing shoulder seam is placed at the same point as the
  garment's. A shaped facing follows the closed dart; an all-in-one facing is drawn after closing darts.
  Lining: copy the garment pieces, remove the facing width, add a 2 cm ease pleat at centre back, hem
  2 to 3 cm shorter than the garment (transformations.md). Source: skill transformations.md.
- **Drafting**: 1) Trace the garment edge and mark the facing width. 2) Match shoulder seam positions.
  3) Lining: trace the garment, subtract the facing, add the pleat, length ease and hem.
- **Sewing order**: facing to garment at the neckline, understitch, turn; attach lining to the facing
- **Finishes and notions**: interfacing on facings, understitching, lining hanging free at the hem
- **Fabrics**: lining in silk, polyester, viscose; facing in the main fabric
- **Fit faults**: facing pops out: understitch and tack; lining pulls: add length and ease.
- **Variants**: #jacket-block, #coat-block
- **CLO3D**: lining as a second layer; set layer order.
- **Confidence**: skill references: trade practice.
- **Status**: practice
