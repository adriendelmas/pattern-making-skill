# Dress Trousers {#dress-trousers}

- **Aliases**: suit trousers, pleated trousers, formal trousers (fr: pantalon de costume)
- **Family**: tailored trousers
- **Recognize**: a center crease, forward pleats or a flat front, side pockets slanted, two jetted back pockets (one or two), a waistband with side adjusters or belt loops, a cuff (turn-up) or a plain hem, a fly with a hook-and-bar.
- **Pieces**: as #mens-trousers plus pleats (in the front pieces), a side-adjuster tab pair (2), a lining for the front to knee (optional), a hem facing (optional), a cuff addition (as a hem allowance).
- **Shape**: Unverified. Front piece with one or two forward pleats from the waist: pleat position at the crease line or at the dart line, pleat intake 3 to 4 cm single or 2 x 2.5 cm double (technique sheet B2); the crease line is the grain line. Pocket opening at the side seam, 18 to 22 cm for men (sheet B8). The back has one or two darts or none, with a jetted pocket 14 to 15 cm wide (typical). Leg opening flat 21 to 23 cm (typical); with a cuff, hem allowance equals 2 x cuff height + 2 cm (cuff 4 to 4.5 cm typical).
- **Drafting**: as #mens-trousers; pleat slashed along the crease and spread by the intake; the waistband extended; side adjusters at the back-waist at the side seam.
- **Sewing order**: as #mens-trousers; pleats before pockets; cuff sewn last.
- **Finishes and notions**: waistband interfacing, curtain, hook and bar, 14 to 16 mm trouser buttons, linings for the knee area.
- **Fabrics**: worsted wool, flannel, tropical wool; ease shrinking to hold the crease; stretch wools reduce ease.
- **Fit faults**: pleats gape -> intake too large for the hip; pleats pull -> depth too small; sagging seat -> back rise too long.
- **Variants**: flat-front, slim, high-waist.
- **CLO3D**: pleats via fold arrangements; set a stiff fabric; press crease by a fold line.
- **Confidence**: technique sheet B2 (typical); trade practice.
