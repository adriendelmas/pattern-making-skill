# Bib Overalls (Dungarees) {#bib-overalls}

- **Aliases**: dungarees, bib-and-brace, carpenter overalls, waist overalls with bib (fr: salopette, cotte à bretelles); (de: Latzhose)
- **Family**: workwear, trousers
- **Recognize**: trousers with a rectangular bib over the chest, two shoulder straps (crossing or parallel at the back) fastened with buckles, buttons or hooks at the bib corners, side openings or a front fly, a bib pocket; carpenter models add a hammer loop on one leg and a rule pocket on the other.
- **Pieces**: bib (1, plus lining or facing 1), bib pocket (1), strap (2), back waistband or yoke (1), front waistband (1), front trouser (2), back trouser (2), side pocket bags (2 pairs), back patch pocket (1 or 2), hammer loop (1), rule pocket (1), side opening strips (2), buckles or buttons.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. One published pattern hack (readytosew.fr, see Sources) drafts the bib as a rectangle, the straps as rectangles 6 cm wide (cut), a back loop as a trapezoid about 10 cm at the base x 7 cm at the top x 10 cm high, and the front and back waistbands narrowed to 7.5 cm; treat these as one worked example, not a standard. General shape (trade practice): a trouser block with a high rise (typical +4 to 6 cm) cut at the waist line; the bib is a rectangle 26 to 32 cm wide x 24 to 30 cm tall (typical), its lower edge sewn to the front waistband at CF; the top corners carry the buckle or button; the straps run from the bib top corners over the shoulders to the back waistband, either crossing at about mid-back or fixed parallel at 8 to 12 cm each side of CB (typical). The back is a high-waist trouser back or a yoke piece. Hammer loop: a small strip sewn on the side seam of one leg; rule pocket: a narrow tall patch pocket on the other thigh.
- **Drafting**: 1. Trouser block (B1 of the technique sheet) with hip ease 10 to 16 cm for workwear (typical); raise CF and CB by 4 to 6 cm for the bib line. 2. Bib width = chest width at the front minus 4 to 8 cm (it sits between the arms). 3. Strap length from the bib corner over the shoulder to the back: measure on the body and add 8 to 12 cm for the adjuster. 4. Because the garment must pass over the hips, add side openings (placket 15 to 20 cm) or a front fly. 5. Pocket bags as in B8. 6. Bar-tack the hardware attachments.
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets (bib, side, back, rule). 2. Hammer loop. 3. Bib faced and topstitched; straps made and turned. 4. Inseams, crotch and side seams. 5. Waistband seam with the bib and back attachments sandwiched. 6. Hardware and straps. 7. Side openings, hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled seams, double-needle topstitch, bar tacks at strap and pocket stress points, buckles or snaps or rivets, buttonholes 8 to 10 mm on strap buttons.
- **Fabrics**: denim, duck canvas, drill; heavy fabrics need grading of the layers at the bib corners.
- **Fit faults**: bib gapes -> straps too long or bib wider than the chest; straps slide -> cross them or bring the attachments closer; seat tight when bending -> add back rise.
- **Variants**: #coverall-boiler-suit, #salopettes-ski, #apron-bib (no trousers).
- **CLO3D**: sew the bib to the waistband first and place the straps over the avatar shoulders; straps as fixed-width pieces; stiff fabric for the bib.
- **Confidence**: piece list: trade practice plus the cited hack. All proportions typical or from that one example; unverified as a drawn pattern.
