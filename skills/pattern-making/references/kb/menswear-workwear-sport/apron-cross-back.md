# Cross-Back Apron {#apron-cross-back}

- **Aliases**: Japanese-style apron, crossback apron, strap-back apron (fr: tablier croisé dans le dos, tablier à bretelles croisées)
- **Family**: workwear, aprons
- **Recognize**: an apron whose two shoulder straps start at the top corners of the bib, pass over the shoulders, cross at the upper back and attach to the waist at the back or at the side edges; no neck strap.
- **Pieces**: apron body (1), straps (2), waist ties (2), pocket (1 or 2), optional bib facing.
- **Shape**: Unverified. Body proportions as #apron-bib. Straps: two long strips, 3 to 5 cm finished width, each 90 to 120 cm cut length (typical): one end is sewn to a bib top corner, the strap runs over the shoulder, crosses the other strap at about 10 to 15 cm below the nape (typical) and ends at the opposite side seam at the waist or at a back waist loop. Alternative fastenings: D-ring, buckle or tie at the back waist. Sew rule: strap -> bib corner reinforced with a box-and-cross stitch or a bar tack.
- **Drafting**: strap length = from the bib corner over the shoulder, diagonally to the opposite hip at the back, plus 10 cm; check the cross point by pinning on the body.
- **Sewing order**: as #apron-bib, with the straps attached at the back waist (loop or caught in the side seam).
- **Finishes and notions**: bar tacks, D-ring or buckle (optional).
- **Fabrics**: linen, cotton canvas, drill, denim.
- **Fit faults**: straps slide off the shoulders -> cross point too low; apron pulls down at the waist -> straps too short.
- **Variants**: #apron-bib, #apron-waist.
- **CLO3D**: place the apron on the avatar, then attach the straps at the back and adjust.
- **Confidence**: trade practice; typical, unverified.
