# Men's Trouser Fly, Waistband and Curtain {#mens-trouser-fly-waistband}

- **Aliases**: fly front, zip fly, waistband with curtain, extension waistband (fr: braguette, ceinture de pantalon, doublure de ceinture)
- **Family**: trouser construction elements
- **Recognize**: a vertical zipper concealed under a flap at the CF of the trousers, a waistband that extends past the CF to carry a button and hook, an inner waistband lining (curtain) visible when the trousers are open.
- **Pieces**: fly facing (1), fly shield (1), right-front fly extension (grown-on), zipper (1), waistband outer (1), waistband curtain (1, lining-fabric strip), waistband interfacing (1), hook and bar.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. Convention: the left front overlaps the right at the CF as worn (trade practice, check the reference). The left front has a separate J-shaped fly facing sewn on its CF edge, lower end rounded, with the topstitch curve (the J) 3.2 to 3.5 cm from CF at the bottom (technique sheet B3, jeans). The right front has a grown-on extension of about 3.5 to 4 cm beyond CF, to which the zipper tape is sewn; the fly shield (folded strip 6 to 8 cm wide) sits behind the zipper. The waistband is a rectangular strip ending at CF with an extension of about 3 to 4 cm on one side for the hook and bar (extension tab) or a straight end; the curtain is a strip of the same length stitched to the waistband seam and folded inside to hide the seam allowances.
- **Drafting**: fly length = zipper length + 1.5 cm (sheet B3), 16 to 18 cm for men; zipper stop at the fly base; shield width 6 to 8 cm; waistband height 3.5 to 4.5 cm; waistband length = W + ease + extension; curtain height = waistband height + 2 SA.
- **Sewing order**: facing to the left front; zipper to the right extension; shield; topstitch the J curve through facing and front; join CF seams to the fly stop; waistband with interfacing; curtain sewn to the waist seam; waistband closed at the ends; hook and bar.
- **Finishes and notions**: grip tape on the curtain (optional), bar tacks at the base of the fly, interfacing 1.5 cm past CF (technique: interfacing end caught to the shield).
- **Fabrics**: heavier fabrics need a bulk-reducing shield made of lining.
- **Fit faults**: fly gapes -> zipper too long or waistband short; fly bubbles -> facing longer than the front; waistband rolls -> missing interfacing or too narrow.
- **Variants**: button fly (jeans, 5 to 6 buttons on the facing), extended waistband (tab) vs flat band, French bearer waistband.
- **CLO3D**: the zipper as a trim; the fly shield/facing as separate pieces with layer order.
- **Confidence**: trade practice. Web results (minervapatterns, craftstorming, nimi CITS manual) confirm the parts and that the fly lap is conventional and handed; exact drafting numbers are from the technique sheet (jeans).
