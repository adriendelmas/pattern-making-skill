# Chef Jacket (Chef's Coat) {#chef-jacket}

- **Aliases**: chef coat, executive chef jacket, double-breasted kitchen jacket, cook's jacket (fr: veste de cuisine, veste de chef); (de: Kochjacke)
- **Family**: workwear, kitchen uniforms
- **Recognize**: a double-breasted front with a wide overlap, two parallel columns of buttons (cloth knot buttons or press studs), a mandarin (stand) collar, long sleeves usually with a turn-up cuff, a small pocket on the upper left sleeve (thermometer or pen pocket), a chest pocket (often left), often a vented or mesh back and underarm vents.
- **Pieces**: front right and left (2, each with a wide grown-on or separate overlap/facing panel), back (1 or 2 with a CB seam; mesh or vented panel 1 optional), sleeve (2, often drop-shoulder one-piece), mandarin collar stand outer and inner (2), sleeve pocket (1), chest pocket (1), cuff (2, turn-up or cut-on), underarm gusset or vent panels (2, optional), inner reversible flap (optional), buttons or studs, interfacing.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. Retail listings describe a drop-shoulder, wide overlapping flaps and buttons in two columns, a left-sleeve utility pocket and inside collar buttons (see Sources); no piece drawing or overlap width was found. Typical geometry (trade practice, unverified): each front is a long rectangular piece whose CF edge extends beyond CF by 12 to 18 cm (the overlap; wearer's right front under or over the left depending on the style), the extension doubled as a facing and interfaced; the two button columns sit about 8 to 12 cm each side of CF on the overlap; the neckline is a shallow round, finished with a stand collar 3 to 5 cm high; the back is a straight rectangle with a slightly curved shoulder, hem even with the front. Sleeve: wide, low cap (drop shoulder), the cap sewn into a deep armhole; cuff turned back (cut as part of the sleeve, folded) 6 to 8 cm. Sew rule: front shoulder -> back shoulder; collar stand lower edge -> neckline; sleeve cap -> armhole; facing hem edge -> front hem.
- **Drafting**: 1. Men's shirt or jacket block with chest ease 24 to 36 cm (typical). 2. Add the double-breasted overlap to CF (12 to 18 cm) and drop the neckline to a crossed V or a round neck. 3. Drop the shoulder 2 to 5 cm, cap height 6 to 10 cm. 4. Length to mid-hip or longer (typical 75 to 85 cm). 5. Sleeve length plus 6 to 8 cm for the turn-up. 6. Sleeve pocket 7 to 8 cm wide x 14 to 16 cm tall at the upper left arm (typical).
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets. 2. Overlap facings. 3. Shoulders. 4. Collar stand. 5. Sleeves. 6. Side and underarm seams. 7. Cuffs. 8. Hem. 9. Buttonholes for the column buttons; knot buttons sewn on loops.
- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled or overlocked seams, heavy bar tacks, press studs or cloth knot buttons (loops are folded cord), inside collar buttons (as noted on the listings), reinforced sleeve pocket.
- **Fabrics**: cotton drill or twill, poly-cotton; high heat and wash resistance; mesh back panel for ventilation.
- **Fit faults**: front gapes when seated -> overlap too narrow; collar tight -> stand shorter than the neck; sleeve pulls at the elbow -> add elbow ease.
- **Variants**: #chef-trousers, #scrubs-top (V-neck), #lab-coat (single-breasted).
- **CLO3D**: layer the overlap panels (inner and outer fronts) and sew the facing; heavy cotton preset; knot buttons as trims.
- **Confidence**: features: retail listings (cited). Overlap and other numbers: typical, unverified as a drawn pattern.
