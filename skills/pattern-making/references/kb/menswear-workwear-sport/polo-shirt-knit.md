# Polo Shirt (Knit) {#polo-shirt-knit}

- **Aliases**: tennis shirt, pique polo, golf shirt base (fr: polo, polo piqué)
- **Family**: knit tops, men's tops
- **Recognize**: knit shirt with a flat-knit or self-fabric collar, a short placket with 2 or 3 buttons, short sleeves with a rib cuff or a plain hem, side vents.
- **Pieces**: front (1, with a slit marked), back (1, often with a back neck tape), sleeve (2), collar (flat knit x1 ready-made, or self-fabric upper and under x2), placket (1 or 2 strips), cuffs (2, optional), neck tape (1), interfacing for placket.
- **Shape**: drawn pattern: not available; the structure follows the technique sheet 4.2 (Knits). Placket slit: 10 to 12 cm in the front center (adult), 8 to 10 cm women; collar: neckline length from placket edge to placket edge x 0.97 to 1.0; collar height 7 to 9 cm when folded; flat-knit collar 36 to 42 cm (supplier-fixed). Sew rule: placket strip wraps the slit and is closed at the bottom with a box stitch; collar neck edge -> neckline, with the collar ends meeting the placket edges.
- **Drafting**: T-shirt block (#mens-tshirt-block) with neckline drop 8 to 9 cm, placket slit, sleeve cap 10 to 13 cm; side vents 5 to 6 cm; hem back longer than front by 2 to 3 cm (typical).
- **Sewing order**: shoulders taped; placket made; shoulders sewn; collar attached; sleeves flat; side seams; vents; hem; buttons.
- **Finishes and notions**: 12 to 14 mm polo buttons (typical), coverstitched hem, shoulder tape 6 mm.
- **Fabrics**: cotton piqué, jersey, performance polyester; stretch in piqué is moderate (typical 20 to 40 percent).
- **Fit faults**: placket twists -> off-grain strip or stretched; collar stands away -> collar too long (use 97 percent of the neckline); collar points curl -> interface the upper collar or use stays.
- **Variants**: golf polo #golf-polo, rugby shirt #rugby-shirt, Henley #henley-placket-shirt.
- **CLO3D**: stretch knit preset with the measured stretch; the collar as a separate piece with a stiffer fabric; shrink the pattern pieces for negative ease if close.
- **Confidence**: technique sheet 4.2 (typical from published practice).
