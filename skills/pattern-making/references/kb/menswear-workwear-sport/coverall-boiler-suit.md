# Coverall / Boiler Suit {#coverall-boiler-suit}

- **Aliases**: boilersuit, overalls (UK sense), mechanic's coverall, work jumpsuit (fr: combinaison de travail, bleu de travail une pièce); (de: Overall, Arbeitsoverall)
- **Family**: workwear, one-piece garments
- **Recognize**: a one-piece garment from collar to ankle with sleeves; a full-length CF zip (often under a storm flap) or a button placket; a flat or stand-fall collar; chest, hip and leg pockets; often an elasticated or half-belted back waist and cuffs and ankles that close with straps, elastic or buttons.
- **Pieces**: front left and front right (2, each runs from the shoulder to the ankle, or a bodice plus a trouser front joined at a waist seam), back (1 or 2 with a CB seam), back yoke (1, optional), sleeve (2), collar upper and under (2) or stand and fall, zip facing or tape strip (2), storm flap (1), chest pocket (1 or 2), side pocket bags (2 pairs), thigh or rule pocket (1, optional), back waist belt or elastic panel (1), cuff tabs (2), interfacing.
- **Shape**: Unverified as a drawn pattern. Construction follows `techniques/skirts-trousers.md` B6 (bodice and trouser blocks placed with the waist lines level, torso lengthened by the jumpsuit girth). Body: trouser block below the waist line and bodice block above it, traced as one piece per front half when there is no waist seam (the waist line stays only as a reference line); the CF edge is straight and carries the zip; the side seam runs unbroken from the underarm to the hem, with an on-seam pocket opening 18 to 22 cm long at hip level (typical); the back crotch point is deeper than on a normal trouser because of the torso lift (technique sheet: torso addition 4 to 6 cm in total, +2 cm if the wearer sits a lot). Back: one piece with a CB seam (to follow the spine) or on a fold, with a yoke seam and an action pleat or an elastic panel at the waist. Sew rule: front crotch seams open to the zip stop, then front -> back at the side seams and inseams; front shoulder -> back shoulder; sleeve cap -> armhole; zip tape -> front facing or CF edge (zip length 50 to 65 cm for a boiler suit, technique sheet B6).
- **Drafting**: 1. Take the girth from the nape over the shoulder to the crotch and back to the nape (jumpsuit girth); the block length from the neck to the crotch, front plus back, equals this plus 4 to 6 cm of ease. 2. Chest ease 20 to 30 cm, hip ease 16 to 24 cm (typical for workwear, room for layers). 3. Waist circumference may be 4 to 8 cm above W (blouson) or elasticated at the back. 4. Opening: CF zip 50 to 65 cm; a protective suit may add a sit-down zip in the crotch. 5. Sleeve cap 10 to 13 cm with a deeper armhole for mobility; add an action pleat at the back (2 to 3 cm). 6. Hem allowance 3 to 4 cm; ankle opening 20 to 24 cm flat for boot clearance (typical).
- **Sewing order**: 1. Pockets on the front pieces and legs. 2. Zip to the CF with the storm flap. 3. Shoulder seams, collar to neck. 4. Sleeves set flat. 5. Side seams and sleeve underarm seams. 6. Inseams and crotch seam. 7. Back waist elastic or belt. 8. Cuffs and hems. 9. Bar tacks at stress points: zip base, pocket corners, crotch.
- **Finishes and notions**: flat-felled or double-needle seams at crotch, side and underarm; bar tacks; heavy zip with a metal or plastic chain; snaps or buttons for the cuffs; reflective trim where the job requires it.
- **Fabrics**: cotton drill or twill, poly-cotton, flame-retardant treated cotton, ripstop, denim; stretch woven reduces the torso addition (about 2 to 3 cm less, typical). Pre-shrink cottons.
- **Fit faults**: pulls at the shoulders when sitting or bending -> torso too short, add 2 cm at the waist or deepen the back crotch; crotch cuts -> crotch curve short; back lifts when reaching -> add an action pleat or back width; legs drag -> hem too long for the boots.
- **Variants**: bib overalls #bib-overalls, ski bib trousers #salopettes-ski, scrub set #scrubs-top, work jacket #work-jacket.
- **CLO3D**: build bodice and trouser as one piece per half, sew the crotch last, test with a seated pose; zip as a trim; stiff woven fabric preset; mesh 10 mm.
- **Confidence**: method: technique sheet B6 (textbook ranges, jumpsuit girth). Piece list and numbers: trade practice, typical, unverified as a drawn pattern.
