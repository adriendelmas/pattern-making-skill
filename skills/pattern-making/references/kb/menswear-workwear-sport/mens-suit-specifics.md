# Men's Suit Specifics {#mens-suit-specifics}

- **Aliases**: two-piece suit, three-piece suit, lounge suit (fr: costume homme, veston)
- **Family**: tailoring, men's
- **Recognize**: a jacket (notch or peak lapel, two or three buttons, flap or jetted pockets, vents) with matching trousers (and waistcoat for three-piece).
- **Pieces**: jacket: front (2), side body (2), back (2, with a CB seam), top sleeve (2), under sleeve (2), top collar (1 or 2), undercollar (1 or 2, melton, cut on the bias), front facing (2), back neck facing (1), pocket flaps and welts (per style), lining and interlining; trousers: see #dress-trousers; waistcoat see #mens-waistcoat.
- **Shape**: Unverified in this file; the full tailoring drafting is in `techniques/tailoring-outerwear.md`. Men-specific: the jacket front has a front dart or a fish dart combined with the front-to-side-body seam (the side body piece sits between front and back, it is a narrow vertical panel from the armhole to the hem, 8 to 12 cm wide at the hem, typical), the lapel gorge line is the seam joining collar to lapel, the lapel rolls on a roll line from the first button to the break point, a canvas and chest piece (hair canvas) shapes the chest. Sew rules: front -> side body -> back seams; shoulder seam; collar to neckline at the gorge; two-piece sleeve joined at forearm and hindarm; vent: center vent (one overlap) or side vents (two) at the back hem.
- **Drafting**: from the men's jacket block; the lapel width 8 to 10 cm (notch) or 9 to 11 cm (peak) is typical, unverified; the vent length 20 to 25 cm.
- **Sewing order**: see tailoring entries; typical: canvas and chest piece, pad stitching of the lapel, pockets, body assembly, collar, sleeves, lining, vents.
- **Finishes and notions**: hair canvas, felt for the undercollar, shoulder pads, sleeve heads, buttons for the cuffs (working or decorative), lining; hand padding.
- **Fabrics**: worsted wool, flannel, tweed; the pattern must account for nap, stripes, ease shrinkage.
- **Fit faults**: collar gap at the neck (stand-away) -> more roll or more neck seam length; drag lines at the sleeve -> pitch; shoulder bridging -> pad too thick.
- **Variants**: tuxedo with a shawl collar; half-canvas vs full-canvas vs fused.
- **CLO3D**: jacket with layers (shell, canvas, lining), heavy fabric parameters, sleeve pitched; pad stitching not modelled.
- **Confidence**: from memory/trade practice; tailoring file is the main source.
