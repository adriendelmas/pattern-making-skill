# Sarong / pareo {#sarong-pareo}

- **Aliases**: sarung, kain, lava-lava (Samoa), pareu or pāreu (Tahiti), lungi (South Asia, tube); fr: paréo
- **Family**: Southeast Asian and Pacific wrapped cloth
- **Recognize**: a rectangle of cloth wrapped around the waist or the chest and tucked or knotted; the Indonesian and Malaysian sarong is a sewn tube, the pareo is an unsewn rectangle.
- **Pieces**: cloth (1 rectangle, sewn into a tube for a sarong; unsewn for a pareo), optional border
- **Shape**: The sarong is a rectangle sewn along the short edges (a closed tube); the pareo is an open rectangle that the wearer wraps and knots. Typical commercial sizes are about 1.0-1.1 m wide and 1.7-2.0 m long (from memory; no source read), and a hand-wrapped textile may be of any width. There is no cutting beyond the rectangle. Unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Width = height from waist to ankle (for a skirt) or from underarm to knee (for a chest wrap); 2. length = hip circumference x 1.5-2 for a wrap (typical); 3. no shaping.
- **Sewing order**: sarong: 1. sew the short edges together; 2. hem the long edges. Pareo: 1. hem all four edges.
- **Finishes and notions**: hemmed edges, fringe or a woven border; no closure.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, rayon, batik, ikat, silk.
- **Fit faults**: falls open -> wider overlap; hem drags -> cut shorter.
- **Variants**: #kimono (wrap), #hakama.
