# Boubou {#boubou}

- **Aliases**: grand boubou, mbubb (Wolof), agbada (Yoruba, three-piece variant), babariga (Hausa); fr: boubou
- **Family**: West African robes
- **Recognize**: very wide, long robe with a large neck opening and wide open sides, embroidered at the neck and chest, worn by men and women with different neck shapes (round for women, V for men) over trousers or a wrapper.
- **Pieces**: body (1, a single rectangle folded in half), neck facing (1), optional embroidered panel (1), optional pocket (1)
- **Shape**: Documented by the Fashion Institute of Technology fashion history entry: the boubou is made of one large rectangle of fabric with an opening in the centre for the neck; the fabric is usually about 150 cm (59 in) wide and of varying length; the grand boubou typically uses a piece about 300 cm (117 in) long and reaches the ankles. Traditional construction: fold the fabric in half, fashion the neck opening, and sew the sides halfway up to make flowing sleeves (the unsewn part is the sleeve opening). The neck for women is wide and round; for men it forms a long V. Museum strip-woven examples (Penn Museum, Liberia and Nigeria) join strips of narrow loom cloth: one Liberian boubou is made of 4.25 in (about 10.8 cm) strips, one Nigerian example of 2.375 in (about 6 cm) strips. Source: FIT fashion history (boubou); Penn Museum object records.
- **Drafting**: 1. Cloth width = body width (about 150 cm). 2. Length = 2 x garment length (fold at shoulder), about 300 cm for a grand boubou. 3. Neck slit or V at the fold, set by the wearer. 4. Sew the sides from the hem up to the underarm opening; the open part at the top forms the sleeve. 5. Strips: the body width divided by the strip width gives the strip count (for example, 150 cm / 10.8 cm = about 14 strips; arithmetic only).
- **Sewing order**: 1. fold at the shoulder line. 2. cut the neck opening. 3. face or bind the neck. 4. side seams to the underarm opening. 5. hem. 6. embroidery on the chest (usually done on the flat cloth before sewing).
- **Finishes and notions**: dense hand or machine embroidery at the neck and chest, bound edges, no closure.
- **Fabrics**: bazin riche (damask cotton), lace, cotton, strip-woven cloth.
- **Fit faults**: neck falls off one shoulder -> narrow the neck opening; sleeves feel short -> raise the side-seam stop.
- **Variants**: #kaftan, #dashiki, #poncho.
- **CLO3D**: single rectangle with a neck hole, sides sewn from the hem to the underarm; a stiffer bending preset for bazin.
- **Confidence**: documented (FIT; museum records); finished dimensions vary by wearer.
- **Status**: practice
