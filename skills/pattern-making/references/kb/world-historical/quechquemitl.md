# Quechquemitl {#quechquemitl}

- **Aliases**: quechquemetl, quexquemitl, quezquemetl; fr: quechquémitl
- **Family**: Mesoamerican rectangular garments (Mexico)
- **Recognize**: a shoulder cape that hangs in points at the front and back (diamond or triangular form), with a head opening; two kinds: a single rectangle folded and sewn, or two rectangles joined.
- **Pieces**: rectangle (2, or 1 folded), neck binding (optional)
- **Shape**: Documented in words: the quechquemitl usually consists of two pieces of rectangular cloth, often handwoven, sewn together to form a poncho-like cape; the head opening is a virtual square formed by joining the two lengths. The simplest method folds a rectangle in half to make a square, sews up the shoulders and leaves an opening for the neck. In some forms the cloth is twisted before the edges are sewn, giving a shape that follows the body more closely. A modern sewing tutorial uses two pieces 14.5 x 27 inches (about 37 x 69 cm); this is a tutorial size, not a loom width. Source: Wikipedia "Quechquemitl"; Oaxaca Culture articles; the exact geometry of the joint varies by community and is unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Each rectangle about 1:2 in proportion (the tutorial uses 37 x 69 cm). 2. Join the two rectangles along part of one long edge so that the unjoined part forms the head opening. 3. Width and length by wearer.
- **Sewing order**: 1. join the rectangles; 2. finish the head opening; 3. hem or fringe.
- **Finishes and notions**: embroidery, tassels, bound edges.
- **Fabrics**: wool, cotton, handwoven textiles.
- **Fit faults**: slips -> narrow the opening; points drag -> shorten the long sides.
- **Variants**: #huipil, #poncho.
- **CLO3D**: two rectangles joined to form an L or diamond; drape on the avatar.
- **Confidence**: documented in words; geometry unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
