# Lederhosen {#lederhosen}

- **Aliases**: Bavarian leather trousers, Kniebundhose (knee-length), Kurze (short); fr: lederhose
- **Family**: Alpine folk workwear and costume
- **Recognize**: leather shorts or knee breeches with a front fall (Latz) or a bib-front, suspenders (Hosentraeger) with a cross-piece (Steg) embroidered on the chest, decorative stitching on the legs and pockets, side or front pocket, tight at the knee in the Kniebund version.
- **Pieces**: front panel (2), back panel (2), seat/crotch gusset (1), front fall or bib / Latz (1), waistband (1), suspenders with cross-piece (2 + 1), pockets (2-3), knee band (2, for long versions)
- **Shape**: Constructed from the shaped hides, not from rectangles: two front legs and two back legs joined with a centre seam and an inner seam, a gusset set into the crotch for movement, the front fall closed with buttons (or a bib on suspenders). The waistband is buttoned. Source: general knowledge; no drawn pattern was read. Unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Use a trouser block (see #trouser-block) with a high rise and a straight leg. 2. Cut the leg length at knee (shorts) or just below the knee (Kniebund). 3. Allow a crotch gusset. 4. Allow seam allowances for leather (6-10 mm; typical).
- **Sewing order**: 1. pockets; 2. inseam; 3. centre seams; 4. crotch gusset; 5. fall or bib; 6. waistband; 7. suspenders.
- **Finishes and notions**: hand or machine decorative stitching, hand-turned edges, horn or metal buttons.
- **Fabrics**: deer, goat or cow suede or leather; leather does not stretch, so the fit has to be right in the toile; textile alternatives for non-leather tests.
- **Fit faults**: rise too short -> lengthen at the centre seam; leather creases at the knee -> add ease.
- **Variants**: #mens-shorts, #trouser-block.
- **CLO3D**: use a leather preset (heavy, low stretch); keep the seat gusset; the pocket flaps by hand.
