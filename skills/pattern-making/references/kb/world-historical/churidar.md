# Churidar {#churidar}

- **Aliases**: churidar pyjama, churidar leggings; fr: churidar
- **Family**: South Asian trousers
- **Recognize**: very narrow leg with extra length that bunches in horizontal folds above the ankle, a short slit or zip at the ankle, tight from knee to ankle, drawstring or yoke waist.
- **Pieces**: leg (2), waist casing or yoke (1), ankle placket (2), drawstring (1)
- **Shape**: A fitted trouser leg with thigh ease and a slender calf and ankle. The leg is cut much longer than the wearer's leg so that it bunches at the ankle. Indian tailoring texts describe cutting it on the bias for stretch along the leg; the ankle opening is a short slit closed with buttons, hooks or a zip. Source: general South Asian tailoring practice; no dimensioned drawing read. Unverified. The extra length for ruching is variable (not known; fitted on the wearer).
- **Drafting**: 1. Measure waist, hip, rise, thigh, knee, calf, ankle. 2. Draft a close-fitting trouser leg narrowing from knee to ankle. 3. Add extra length for the bunching, set by test on the wearer. 4. Allow a slit at the outside ankle.
- **Sewing order**: 1. inseams. 2. outer seams to the slit. 3. crotch seam. 4. waist. 5. ankle slit and closure. 6. hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip or hooks at the ankle, drawstring or elastic.
- **Fabrics**: cotton, cotton-lycra, silk; a bias cut gives stretch; stretch fabrics reduce the need for the bias.
- **Fit faults**: tight at the knee -> add width at the knee; ruching drags -> shorten the extra length.
- **Variants**: #salwar (full), #kameez.
- **CLO3D**: two legs sewn; the pattern is longer than the avatar leg; the ruching comes from simulation and shirring, not from the pattern.
