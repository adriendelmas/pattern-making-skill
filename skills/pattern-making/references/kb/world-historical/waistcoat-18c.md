# 18th-century waistcoat {#waistcoat-18c}

- **Aliases**: vest (Am.), weskit; fr: gilet, veste (18th c.)
- **Family**: Historical men's garments
- **Recognize**: sleeveless, close-fitting garment worn under the coat, with front buttons from the neck to the hem, flaps and pockets, front pieces of fine or embroidered cloth and back pieces of plain linen.
- **Pieces**: front (2, of fashion fabric), back (2 or 1, of plain linen), lining (front lining 2), pocket flap (2), pocket bag (2), button and buttonhole strip, back lacing or buckle (optional)
- **Shape**: Unverified in this entry; general knowledge. 18th-century waistcoats were cut with fronts of the outer cloth (often embroidered to shape on the flat) and cheaper backs; they shortened over the century. A V&A record links the shortening of the waistcoat to the change in breeches closure (fly front to fall front). The shape (fronts with a curved hem, a back with a centre seam and a lacing or buckle adjustment) is known from general sources only. No drawn pattern was read in this entry; Arnold, Patterns of Fashion 1 (1660-1860) includes menswear patterns.
- **Drafting**: Not known here.
- **Sewing order**: unverified.
- **Finishes and notions**: covered or metal buttons, handworked buttonholes, lining.
- **Fabrics**: silk, wool; linen for the back and lining.
- **Fit faults**: unverified.
- **Variants**: #mens-waistcoat (modern), #doublet, #breeches-18c.
- **CLO3D**: build as a fitted vest with separate fronts and back; buttons by hand.
- **Confidence**: from memory; shape unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
