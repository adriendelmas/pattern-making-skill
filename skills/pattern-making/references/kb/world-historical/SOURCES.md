# Sources: world-historical

- Format and scope: this base's own FORMAT.md; the first twelve entries (kimono to ao dai) were written in an earlier pass and cite the Bunka Fashion Research Institute yukata pages, Mueller & Sohn notes, Okayama CAD paper on the kimono, UBC Museum of Anthropology records (Korean skirt), fibre2fashion (ao dai).
- Books (not read in full; titles used for scope and confirmed by catalogue records): Janet Arnold, Patterns of Fashion 1 (1660-1860), 2 (1860-1940), 3 (c.1560-1620), 4 (linen shirts, smocks, neckwear, headwear), 5 (bodies, stays, hoops and rumps c.1595-1795, completed by Jenny Tiramani and others, School of Historical Dress 2018); Dorothy K. Burnham, Cut My Cote (Royal Ontario Museum, 1973; contents not verified in this pass); Max Tilke, Costume Patterns and Designs (to check for Maghreb, Ottoman and Asian garments); Fransen, Nørgård and Østergård, Medieval Garments Reconstructed: Norse Clothing Patterns (Aarhus University Press, 2011); Østergård, Woven into the Earth (2004); Sebesta and Bonfante, The World of Roman Costume (to check the toga).
- Museums and institutions: Victoria and Albert Museum records (shirt O13939 and O137719; breeches O15596; cage crinoline O122389 and O58867; tunic O119593; frock coat O13952); Metropolitan Museum essay "The chiton, peplos and himation in modern dress"; Penn Museum object records (huipil, boubou); UBC Museum of Anthropology (blouse Sa331); Maidstone Museum (1850s cage crinoline); Newport Historical Society (cage crinoline); University of New Hampshire Irma G. Bowen Historic Clothing Collection (Regency gown, cage crinoline 1868-1873); Colonial Williamsburg (breeches and shirt records); Fashion Institute of Technology fashion history pages (boubou, cuirass bodice).
- Web pages: scottishdance.net "Making a Kilt"; kilts-n-stuff.com and St Kilda store (kilt yardage); cottesimple.com (grande assiette and 14th-century gores); reconstructinghistory.com (cotehardie and breeches pattern notes); Demode Couture "Francaise 2" (robe a la francaise); fashionthroughherstory.com (Regency round gown); sensibility.com (Regency photo instructions); Oaxaca Culture and Wikipedia "Quechquemitl"; Wikipedia "Dashiki", "Crinoline", "Frock coat"; Brainkart "Kalidhar Kurta" (Indian school textbook, child size); NIMI CTS Dress Making trade practical (saree blouse; pages not read); Minerva Patterns tailcoat descriptions; Past Patterns and Truly Victorian listings (1920s and 1880s bodices).
- Confidence note: many sources above are secondary (maker blogs, shops, summaries); entries that depend on them say "unverified" or "documented in words" and give no invented numbers. Rectangular-garment layouts for Japanese, Korean, Chinese, Vietnamese, Arabian, Maghreb, West African and Mesoamerican garments should be checked against a community-specific drawn pattern before use.
