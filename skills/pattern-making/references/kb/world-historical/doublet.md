# Doublet (16th-century) {#doublet}

- **Aliases**: pourpoint (earlier French), peascod-belly doublet; fr: pourpoint
- **Family**: Renaissance men's garments
- **Recognize**: close-fitting padded jacket reaching the waist, often with a rounded padded belly (peascod), buttons or hooks down the front, a high stand collar, shoulder wings, tabs (skirts) at the waist, and holes or points at the waist to tie the hose.
- **Pieces**: front (2), back (2, with centre back seam), sleeve (2, two-piece), wing (2), collar (1), skirt tab (several), interlining and lining (matching)
- **Shape**: Described in Janet Arnold, Patterns of Fashion 3 (1560-1620), as the cut and construction of clothes for men and women of the period; the doublet is a fitted, quilted or padded garment with lining and interlining, with shoulder wings and tabs, pointed to the hose. Dimensioned patterns exist in that book (I did not have access to the plates). Unverified in this entry.
- **Drafting**: Not known here; follow the pattern of the museum garment being reproduced.
- **Sewing order**: unverified.
- **Finishes and notions**: hand-worked eyelets for points, buttons, hook closures.
- **Fabrics**: silk satin, velvet, linen interlining.
- **Fit faults**: unverified.
- **Variants**: #trunk-hose (worn with it), #waistcoat-18c (later descendant).
- **CLO3D**: fitted jacket; padding by a thick fabric layer or a thicker mesh.
- **Confidence**: documented by title only; shape unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
