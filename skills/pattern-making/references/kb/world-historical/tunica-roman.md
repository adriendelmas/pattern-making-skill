# Tunica (Roman tunic) {#tunica-roman}

- **Aliases**: tunica, tunica manicata (long-sleeved), colobium (sleeveless); fr: tunique romaine
- **Family**: Roman garments
- **Recognize**: knee- or ankle-length T-shaped tunic, sleeveless or short-sleeved, belted at the waist, bands (clavi) down the front for status in some versions.
- **Pieces**: body (1 folded, or front + back as 2 rectangles), sleeve (2, optional), neck slit or hole (cut), clavi bands (optional)
- **Shape**: Two descriptions in the secondary literature: the Victoria and Albert Museum states the construction was similar for men, women and children: one piece of cloth folded over the shoulders and sewn down the sides, becoming two or three pieces with a horizontal seam above the waist from about the 5th century; other popular accounts describe two rectangular panels joined at the shoulders. Tunics found in Egypt are often woven to shape in one piece. Sleeves, where present, are straight rectangles (reenactment sources; hobbyist). Source: V&A tunic record; general sources. Dimensions unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Length from shoulder to knee or ankle. 2. Width at least chest/2 + ease. 3. Neck slit or round hole. 4. Side seams leaving armholes.
- **Sewing order**: 1. shoulders; 2. neck; 3. side seams; 4. hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: belt, tabs.
- **Fabrics**: wool, linen.
- **Fit faults**: under-arm tight -> add underarm gusset; too narrow -> use a wider rectangle.
- **Variants**: #medieval-tunic-gored, #chiton, #kaftan.
- **CLO3D**: two rectangles with a neck opening, sewn at the shoulders and sides; belt.
- **Confidence**: documented (V&A) in words; dimensions unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
