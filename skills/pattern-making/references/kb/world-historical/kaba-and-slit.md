# Kaba and slit {#kaba-and-slit}

- **Aliases**: kaba, kaba and slit (Ghana); fr: kaba et slit
- **Family**: West African two-piece dress
- **Recognize**: fitted blouse (kaba) with a sweetheart, round or square neckline, princess seams and flared peplum or hem, worn with a long fitted skirt (slit) cut straight or tapering, with a slit up the back or side to allow walking.
- **Pieces**: kaba front (2), kaba back (2), kaba sleeve (2), kaba hem frill or peplum (optional), slit skirt front (1), slit skirt back (2, with a slit), waistband (1), facings
- **Shape**: The kaba is a fitted blouse cut like a close-fitting bodice (see #saree-blouse and #close-fitting-bodice-block) with princess seams or darts, extended to the hip or with a peplum. The slit is a straight or slightly tapered skirt (see #pencil-skirt and #straight-skirt) with a vent from the hem upward at the back (or side) and a waistband or yoke. Source: general African dressmaking practice; no drawn pattern read. Unverified.
- **Drafting**: 1. Draft the kaba on a close-fitting bodice block. 2. Draft the slit on a straight skirt block with a vent length set by comfort (typical 20-30 cm at the back). 3. Use a matching fabric so the print runs on the straight grain.
- **Sewing order**: 1. kaba darts and seams. 2. shoulders and facings. 3. sleeves. 4. peplum. 5. skirt seams. 6. vent. 7. waistband. 8. closures.
- **Finishes and notions**: zip, hooks, matching bias binding, interfacing at the waist.
- **Fabrics**: wax print cotton, lace, brocade; strongly printed cloth is matched at the seams and on the centre.
- **Fit faults**: kaba gapes at the bust -> add a bust dart; slit skirt twists -> check the grain.
- **Variants**: #saree-blouse (for the choli bodice), #pencil-skirt, #boubou.
- **CLO3D**: bodice and skirt as separate garments; keep the vent open.
- **Confidence**: from memory; shape unverified.
