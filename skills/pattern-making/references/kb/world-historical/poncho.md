# Poncho {#poncho}

- **Aliases**: ruana (Colombia), unku (Andean tunic, different form), manta; fr: poncho
- **Family**: Andean and South American rectangular garments
- **Recognize**: a large rectangle or two joined rectangles with a slit or hole in the centre for the head, worn hanging from the shoulders; no sleeves; fringed or finished edges.
- **Pieces**: panel (1 or 2, rectangular), neck binding (optional)
- **Shape**: In the Andean form, two woven rectangles are sewn together along most of the long edge, leaving a gap in the middle for the neck; or a single rectangle with a central slit. The rectangles are cut from the loom width. Documented in general descriptions; no dimensioned drawing was read here. Unverified numerically.
- **Drafting**: 1. Width of each rectangle (loom width). 2. Length so that the poncho reaches the hip or the knee. 3. Neck slit about 25-30 cm (typical; verify on the head). 4. Seam allowance for the join.
- **Sewing order**: 1. join the rectangles, leaving the neck gap; 2. bind the neck; 3. fringe or hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: fringe, bound neck.
- **Fabrics**: wool, alpaca, cotton; the weave sets the drape.
- **Fit faults**: slips off the shoulders -> shorten the neck opening; swings -> add side ties.
- **Variants**: #huipil, #quechquemitl, #boubou.
- **CLO3D**: one or two rectangles; cut a neck slit; no side seams.
- **Confidence**: from memory; shape unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
