# Toga {#toga-roman}

- **Aliases**: toga virilis, toga praetexta; fr: toge
- **Family**: Roman draped garments
- **Recognize**: very large semicircular (or oval) wool cloth draped over the left shoulder, around the back, under the right arm and across the chest, with the end thrown over the left shoulder again; no fastenings.
- **Pieces**: semicircle of cloth (1)
- **Shape**: Described in secondary sources as a single length of wool cut as a semicircle and wrapped round the body without fastening; the Greek himation is a rectangle, the Roman toga a semicircle when folded. Dimensions differ strongly between sources: about 5.5 m long and 2.75 m deep at its widest for an imperial toga (popular account); a reconstruction cites about 17 ft from tip to tip and 5 ft broad at the widest; another account cites 6 ells, which is far smaller and looks inconsistent. The depth of the curve changed over time. Source: popular secondary accounts; unverified, and scholarly sources (Sebesta and Bonfante) should be checked.
- **Drafting**: Not known from a verified source. Start from the reported size range (straight edge about 5 m, depth about 1.5-2.75 m depending on the source) and adjust by a trial wrap on the wearer; hem the curve.
- **Sewing order**: hem only.
- **Finishes and notions**: a purple border (toga praetexta) or plain white; no fastenings.
- **Fabrics**: wool.
- **Fit faults**: slides off the left shoulder -> increase the length of the draped end; trips -> shorten the depth.
- **Variants**: #himation, #sarong-pareo.
- **CLO3D**: semicircular panel; place on the avatar with pins or manual arrange; very heavy wool preset.
- **Confidence**: popular sources; dimensions unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
