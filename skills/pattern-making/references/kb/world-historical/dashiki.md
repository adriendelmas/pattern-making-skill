# Dashiki {#dashiki}

- **Aliases**: danshiki, dansiki, kitenge-style shirt (printed cloth); fr: dashiki
- **Family**: West African shirts and tunics
- **Recognize**: loose pullover shirt in a bold print, with a V-neck and an embroidered panel around the neck and chest, short or long sleeves, often side slits.
- **Pieces**: front (1), back (1), sleeve (2), neck facing (1), embroidered panel (1, optional), side pocket (optional)
- **Shape**: A T of rectangles: front and back cut from the cloth width, folded at the shoulder, sleeves straight; the V-neck is a short slit. Side slits are left open at the hem. Source: Wikipedia Dashiki and a museum-style design essay (Momaa) describe the form; no dimensioned layout was read. Unverified. Because the print is directional, pieces cannot be nested freely, which raises fabric use.
- **Drafting**: 1. Width at the chest = (chest + ease)/2. 2. Length by style (hip to mid-thigh for shirts). 3. V-neck depth by style (not known). 4. Slit heights by style.
- **Sewing order**: 1. shoulders. 2. neck facing and embroidery. 3. sleeves flat. 4. side seams with slits. 5. hem.
- **Finishes and notions**: neck embroidery, bound slits.
- **Fabrics**: cotton print, wax print, kitenge.
- **Fit faults**: neck gapes -> shorten the V; underarm drags -> deepen the underarm.
- **Variants**: #boubou, #gandoura, #kurta.
- **CLO3D**: rectangles with a V neck; cotton preset; apply the print as a texture on the front panel.
- **Confidence**: from memory; shape unverified.
- **Status**: unverified
